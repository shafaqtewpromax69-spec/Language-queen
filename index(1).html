Comprehension — 20 marks
Read the passage and answer the questions.

Passage

When the school announced a “quiet hour” after lunch, most students expected the usual kind of silence: a teacher standing nearby and everyone pretending to work. Instead, the library became the centre of the experiment. Students could read, revise, draw diagrams or simply sit without using their phones. At first, many found the hour uncomfortable. Without the constant stream of messages and short videos, even ten minutes seemed unusually long.

By the third day, however, something changed. Several students said they had finished work faster than usual because they were not switching between tasks every few minutes. Others discovered that they actually enjoyed reading when there was no pressure to post about it. The librarian noticed that students were also speaking more quietly when the hour ended, as if the calm had followed them outside.

The experiment was not perfect. Some students still struggled to concentrate, and others simply stared at the clock. Yet the school concluded that the point was not to make every student productive every minute. The real lesson was that attention is easier to protect when distractions are removed deliberately rather than when people merely promise themselves to “try harder.”
